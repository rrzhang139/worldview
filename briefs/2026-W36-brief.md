# Weekly Brief — 2026-W36

## Top developments this week

1. **⚠️ The Hormuz standoff crosses from blockade threat into sustained kinetic exchange — the first direct US strikes on Iranian tankers of this conflict.** Sept 1: US CENTCOM struck ~100 IRGC-linked targets (air defense, radar, mine-laying assets, anti-ship missile/drone launchers), and under a new "tanker-for-tanker" policy hit two Iranian government tankers' engine rooms with drones. Iran retaliated within 24 hours (Sept 2) with ~25 ballistic missiles/drones against US-linked sites in Bahrain, Kuwait, Jordan, and Erbil (~11 reported Iranian casualties). Brent closed **$95.63/bbl Sept 2 (+~9% on the week)**; the 30Y UST held roughly flat at ~5.27%, no acute rates spike yet. **What this updates:** last week's "60-day deadline expired, blockade threatened" framing is overtaken — this is now a recurring direct exchange, not a standoff, the sharpest active-conflict-frequency data point logged this cycle. Oil becomes a second live macro-transmission channel into AI-infra financing costs, alongside the 30Y. Updates `themes/geopolitics-conflicts.md`, `themes/ai-compute-capex.md`.

2. **⚠️ Nvidia's China-revenue line hits a hard number (<1%), and CXMT ships a DRAM generation Micron hasn't commercialized yet.** On the Q2 FY27 call (Aug 26), Nvidia's CFO said licensed H200 shipments to China customers were **<1% of Data Center revenue** — not a US export block, but Beijing's own informal discouragement of purchases, with zero China DC revenue assumed going forward. Separately, **CXMT started mass production of LPDDR6 on Aug 29** (12,800 MT/s, 16GB/die), the first commercial LPDDR6 anywhere, shipping in Xiaomi's 18 Fold — on mature, EUV-free lithography. Taiwan's Keelung prosecutors also indicted an Nvidia Taiwan manager plus 8 others (Aug 24-25) for smuggling 74 B300-equipped servers into China, the first criminal case brought by Taiwan's own judiciary rather than US Commerce/DOJ. **What this updates:** the demand-side substitution this theme has tracked now has a hard number, and it's sharper than "informal blocking" — while CXMT reaching a DRAM generation ahead of the Western leader's ship date is a genuine gap-closing data point, not just a capacity story. Enforcement against smuggling is also now multi-jurisdictional, not just American. Updates `themes/china-tech.md`, `themes/semiconductors.md`.

3. **⚠️ Nuclear restart moves from PPA press release to physical execution, and PJM's curtail-unbacked-load framework draws broad pushback at its own comment deadline.** Aug 31: Holtec began loading fuel into Palisades' reactor vessel (204 assemblies) — the first-ever restart of a fully decommissioned US nuclear plant nearing completion. Sept 3: comments closed on PJM's ER26-3515 large-load framework (logged Aug 17), and the Data Center Coalition, Vistra, PJM's own independent Market Monitor, and a joint Advanced Energy United/SEIA filing all criticized it as unworkable, discriminatory, or reliability-risky. **What this updates:** nuclear restart is now a physically executing supply path with fuel in the vessel, materially upgrading its credibility as a near-term marginal-MW source — while the regulatory rationing framework this theme flagged as the likely near-term answer is now a live, contested fight rather than a settled rule, including pushback from PJM's own market monitor. Updates `themes/energy-systems.md`.

4. **India's Semicon 2.0 reverses the "stalled" baseline; Taiwan's drone budget converts into a firm contract; a US drone tariff goes live.** Aug 31: India fully notified all six pillars of Semicon 2.0 — ₹1,27,500 crore (~$14.4B), a 40% fab-capex subsidy — eleven days after being logged as "no new approvals." Aug 27: Taiwan's Legislative Yuan passed a NT$240B (~$7.3B) six-year drone budget plus a NT$26.9B (~$847M) Anduril contract for 1,554 Altius-700M loitering munitions. Sept 3: the Aug-13 100% Section 232 tariff on large/thermal-imaging drones took effect (sub-25kg at 25%; components delayed to Feb 2027). **What this updates:** three separate industrial-policy tracks all moved from announcement to execution in the same week — India's reversal is the sharpest single data point on this theme's execution-vs-capture question, though whether the subsidy converts to fabs (not just approvals) is still unproven. Updates `themes/industrial-policy-defense.md`, `themes/geopolitics-conflicts.md`.

5. **Broadcom discloses a $350B two-year forward AI backlog — and the market prices it for perfection anyway.** Sept 2: Broadcom's Q3 FY26 print showed AI semiconductor revenue $16.7B (+221% YoY), a raised full-year AI guide to $58B, and CEO Hock Tan disclosed **$350B of contracted AI accelerator/networking shipments to six hyperscalers through FY27-28** ($115B FY27, $230B FY28). The stock still fell **~6%** because the $34.8B Q4 total-revenue guide landed slightly under the Street's $35B. Same day at the G20 Innovation Ministerial, Jensen Huang pitched AI as sovereign infrastructure ($50-60B per GW) to government ministers alongside Sam Altman and Anthropic's Tom Brown. **What this updates:** a two-year contracted backlog is the strongest hard evidence yet that demand is booked, not speculative — but a 6% drop on an in-line-not-beat guide shows equity investors are now pricing continued perfection, tightening the "prove it" bar even as the order book grows and labs court a new sovereign-buyer class beyond hyperscalers. Updates `themes/ai-compute-capex.md`, `themes/tech-investments.md`.
