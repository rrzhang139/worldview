# Weekly Brief — 2026-W18

## Top developments this week

1. **Q1 hyperscaler earnings: AI monetization crosses a threshold. ⚠️ Partial mind-changer on ai-compute-capex.** MSFT Azure AI is now running at $37B annualized (+123% YoY). Google Cloud hit $20B (+63% YoY). AWS $150B run rate (+28%). Combined 2026 capex guide raised again to **$725B** (up from $700B last week, up 77% from 2025's $410B). The bear thesis — "capex with no revenue" — is now definitively wrong. Revenue is real and accelerating. What remains unresolved: whether capex increases *faster* than revenue for long enough to threaten FCF and bond refinancing math. Market reflected this ambiguity live: GOOG up (Cloud growth directly tied to AI); META down ~6% (ad revenue +33% wasn't enough to justify the capex raise narrative). **The new scrutiny variable is capex-growth rate relative to revenue-growth rate**, not just whether revenue exists. Updates `themes/ai-compute-capex.md`.

2. **Capability sprint: GPT-5.5 (Apr 23) + Claude Opus 4.7 (Apr 16) + DeepSeek V4 (Apr 24). Scaling curve still holding.** Three frontier-class releases in two weeks. Release cadence alone is a signal — 6 weeks from GPT-5.4 to GPT-5.5. On SWE-bench Verified (agentic software engineering): **DeepSeek V4 scored 80.6%, GPT-5.5 scored 58.6%** — open-weight Chinese model leading closed-source US frontier on this specific benchmark. On agentic Terminal-Bench: GPT-5.5 leads 82.7% vs V4's 67.9%. NIST CAISI evaluation places V4 ~8 months behind the true frontier. **At 1/7th the inference cost, open-weight, MIT license.** The agentic deployment environment is fracturing into multi-model routing — enterprises picking models per task, no single provider commitment. Updates `themes/ai-models-economics.md`, `themes/tech-investments.md`.

3. **SMIC yield rates and 5nm status — the numbers you asked about.** Huawei Ascend 910C yield at SMIC: **20–40%**. TSMC's comparable-node yield: >90%. This is the clearest single number for "how far behind is China on chips." *What yield means:* on a wafer of 100 chips, SMIC keeps 20–40 usable ones; TSMC keeps 90+. The rest are defective — the materials and fab time are wasted. This is why Chinese AI chips cost dramatically more per usable unit despite lower specs, and why SMIC's capacity expansions (targeting 80K wafers/month by 2027) don't linearly translate to usable chip output. HBM remains the harder bottleneck: SMIC can try to fix yield over time, but HBM requires SK Hynix or Samsung — both export-controlled. SMIC 5nm pilot runs are underway; mass production target remains 2026. Ascend unit output goal: 600K units (2x 2025). Updates `themes/semiconductors.md`, `themes/china-tech.md`.

4. **BIS Jan 2026 rule — H200-tier case-by-case licensing with strict conditions.** Significant policy shift from blanket denial: H200/MI325X-class chips (not Blackwell/frontier tier) can now be exported to China case-by-case, with requirements: US supply sufficiency certification, no foundry capacity diversion, recipient security audit, independent third-party performance testing. 25% Section 232 tariff on same chips. Reading: US slightly relaxing at the generation-behind tier while implicitly holding the line at frontier. NVDA's Q1 guidance explicitly excludes China revenue (~$50B market) — the practical signal is that even with this opening, the demand pathway remains closed for the chips that matter. Updates `themes/ai-policy-regulation.md`.

5. **EU AI Act enforcement powers activate Aug 2, 2026 — 3 months out.** GPAI obligations have been enforceable since Aug 2025; the adjustment window closes Aug 2 and the AI Office gains fine authority (up to 3% global revenue or €15M). This is the first hard regulatory deadline in frontier AI governance. Affected: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta with GPAI models above the threshold. The Code of Practice (final version July 2025) is the compliance path. Updates `themes/ai-policy-regulation.md`.

Software independence is tracking well ahead of hardware independence (SMIC yield tells that story). This is the asymmetry to watch: China may not need chip parity if software-level efficiency keeps closing the gap.

## Cross-links to learning queue

You asked about **FOMC** and **post-ZIRP** via highlight this week. The Fed held at 3.5–3.75% and **Powell's chairmanship ends May 15** — Kevin Warsh, historically hawkish, takes over. While we've removed the monetary-policy theme (it's slow-moving macro), the Warsh transition is a fast-moving event worth watching through the AI lens: if Warsh keeps rates higher-for-longer, the discount rate on AI infrastructure investments rises, capex justification math gets harder, and the bond terms for hyperscaler debt issuances tighten. The direct transmission from Fed policy to AI capex is: rates → cost of hyperscaler bond issuance → CapEx financing cost → how aggressively the $725B guide can realistically be executed. Brief notation only — not enough to warrant re-adding the macro theme.

For **SMIC yield** specifically: the 20–40% number is the critical signal. Map it to the `semiconductors` mental model — *the bottleneck always moves*. Right now for China, the bottleneck is yield (physics/process maturity), then HBM (supply chain), then advanced packaging (tooling). Even if SMIC closes the yield gap over 2-3 years (plausible), HBM remains export-controlled. That's two sequential constraints. The "5x capacity by 2027" headline sounds dramatic; the effective output is yield × capacity, which is much smaller.
