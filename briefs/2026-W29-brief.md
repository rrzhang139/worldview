# Weekly Brief — 2026-W29

## Top developments this week

1. **⚠️ GPT-5.6 Sol scores 7.8% on ARC-AGI-3 — the first real movement off the near-zero baseline that was last week's biggest mind-changer.** Sol jumps from GPT-5.5's 0.43% to 7.8% (13.3% Public / 7.8% Semi-Private), and becomes the first model to outright win a public ARC-AGI-3 game (ft09, 87%). Confirmed directly against arcprize.org's own results pages. **What this updates:** W28 read ARC-AGI-3's under-1%-across-the-board score as "the clearest scaling-plateau signal since tracking began" and flagged it as the theme's mind-changer trigger. One model generation later, the gap moved — a 20× relative jump, though still far from the ~100% human baseline. This doesn't resolve the plateau question (7.8% is still a wide gap), but it does mean the gap is *hard, not frozen* — worth watching whether Gemini/Claude's next releases show a comparable jump, since a single-lab data point isn't yet a trend. Updates `themes/ai-models-economics.md` (mind-changer, partial reversal).

2. **TSMC and ASML both beat Q2 2026 earnings and raise full-year guidance — the upstream end of the AI-capex chain keeps confirming, not softening.** TSMC: $40.2B revenue (+36% YoY), GM 67.7%, advanced nodes 77% of wafer revenue; raises FY2026 capex guidance to $60–64B (from $52–56B) and revenue-growth guidance to "slightly above 40%" YoY; CEO C.C. Wei says the next three years of capex will be "even more significantly higher" than the prior three. ASML: €9.3B net sales, €2.9B net income, GM 54.0%; raises FY2026 outlook to €43–45B — its second guidance hike this year — with China now ~20% of 2026 revenue (share falling, dollars still rising). **What this updates:** answers the W28 open question on TSMC's Q2 print directly, and confirms the "de-risked Rubin ramp" read holds through actual delivered numbers, not just supply-chain rumor. Neither company's call referenced the still-unresolved ASML EUV-leak allegation. Updates `themes/ai-compute-capex.md`, `themes/semiconductors.md`.

3. **Anthropic schedules IPO investor meetings, still targeting an October debut — the first concrete step since the June 1 confidential S-1.** Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and JPMorgan are leading. Separately, Yipit data (via The Information) puts Anthropic's annualized revenue run-rate at ~$69B, up from ~$47B in May, with daily ARR growth accelerating from ~$400M/day to ~$550M/day — though realized calendar-2026 revenue is estimated lower, at $20–26B. **What this updates:** the W28 open question ("does the Anthropic IPO hold its price past 30 days, unlike SPCX?") gets a concrete next milestone but not yet a price to test — investor meetings precede pricing by several weeks. The run-rate acceleration is a genuinely new data point on Anthropic's enterprise-sovereignty moat, independent of IPO timing. Updates `themes/tech-investments.md`.

4. **No resolution on the two loudest open threads from last week: the ASML EUV-leak allegation and the Colossus 2 turbine lawsuit.** The allegation (Commerce Secretary Lutnick's claim that an EUV machine or component reached China, denied by ASML) has had no new developments since late June — still unresolved, evidence undisclosed. The NAACP-backed Clean Air Act suit over unpermitted turbines at SpaceX/xAI's Colossus 2 (the facility Anthropic pays ~$1.25B/month to access) remains pending, with the DOJ's motion-to-dismiss and an injunction hearing both expected in August. **Why flag a non-update:** both are named "what would change my mind" triggers on `china-tech.md` and `tech-investments.md` respectively — worth tracking that neither has moved, rather than letting them fade from attention silently.

5. **Continued military-tempo escalation around Taiwan, no acute trigger.** China and Russia ran "Joint Sea-2026" combined naval drills off Qingdao (July 6–13) alongside a reported Chinese SLBM test; Taiwan stood up a new Littoral Combat Command (July 1) integrating US-supplied missile systems with a new unmanned-surface-vessel unit. Reads as a continuing baseline alongside the June 23 Fujian Strait transit and the standing $14B arms-sale leverage play — worth watching whether Taiwan's new command shows up in any joint exercise with the US, which would be a first for direct interoperability signaling. Updates `themes/geopolitics-conflicts.md`.

## Mind-changers ⚠️

- **ARC-AGI-3 gets its first real movement (item 1).** Not a full reversal of the W28 plateau read — 7.8% is still far from resolving whether interactive/compositional capability tracks the scaling curve — but it changes the read from "frozen" to "hard but moving." The next test: does this jump replicate across other labs' next releases, or was it GPT-5.6-specific?

## Open questions for next week

- **Does GPT-5.6 Sol's ARC-AGI-3 jump replicate across Gemini/Claude's next releases, or is it isolated?** The single biggest thing to watch on the scaling-plateau question.
- **Does the ASML EUV-leak allegation ever get substantiated, or does it formally fade?** Still the largest single trigger on the export-control regime's credibility.
- **Does the Colossus 2 turbine lawsuit's August hearing produce an actual injunction?** Would be the first time the energy-backlash and AI-capex themes collide on a named, disclosed compute contract.
- **When do Anthropic's IPO investor meetings convert to a pricing date, and does the print hold past 30 days** (unlike SPCX)?
- **Hyperscaler Q2 earnings land July 22–30** (Alphabet 7/22, Microsoft/Meta 7/29, Amazon 7/30) — first read on whether capex guidance keeps climbing alongside TSMC's raised numbers, or diverges.

## Cross-links to learning

You asked in May how to read TSMC's "capex curve" (the total-dollars-spent trajectory) versus the "capability-per-dollar" efficiency curve, and separately about NVDA's slip risk ahead of the Vera Rubin ramp. This week's TSMC print is the cleanest example yet of the capex curve itself steepening from *inside* the supply chain rather than from hyperscaler press releases: TSMC didn't just meet its prior $52–56B capex guide, it raised it to $60–64B, and framed the next three years as "even more significantly higher" than the last three — the foundry is now guiding to an accelerating curve, not a flattening one. Read alongside NVDA's still-on-schedule Q3 Rubin ramp (no slip so far), the supply side of the "is this sustainable" question keeps answering "yes, and rising" every time it's tested against an actual earnings print rather than a forecast.
