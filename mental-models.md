# Mental Models — One Lens Per Theme

Each theme has a single load-bearing mental model. Use them as **checkpoints** when reading the news: when you see a development, run it through the model first to filter what actually matters.

These also live inside each theme file under "## Mental model." This page is the browsable index.

---

## AI & Tech

### `ai-compute-capex` — Arms race economics — capex outruns monetization in early phases.

Like railroads, telegraphs, electric grid, fiber. Investment surges *ahead* of demonstrated demand because if it works, first-mover advantage is enormous. **The question is never whether some buildout is wasted — it always is.** Roughly 30% pays off. Companies that survive the shakeout dominate the next era. Read AI capex through this lens: *who survives*, not *whether the cycle was rational*.

### `ai-models-economics` — Scaling laws are religion until they break.

The empirical "more compute → better models" relationship has held for 7+ years. The entire AI capex thesis depends on it continuing. **Every quarter, ask: are we still on the curve?** Watch flagship next-gen releases as the test. Capability gains proportional to compute → curve holds. Sublinear gains → curve cracking. Treat continuation as a probability, not certainty.

### `ai-policy-regulation` — Regulation is industrial policy written in the language of compliance.

A BIS export control rule = a national security strategy expressed as a license requirement. An EU AI Act GPAI obligation = risk management expressed as a transparency mandate. A compute threshold in law = a government's estimate of where "frontier" starts. **Track the regulatory front as a leading indicator of what capabilities nations are afraid of** — regulators are 12–18 months behind the frontier, but their moves reveal what the intelligence community thinks is coming.

### `china-tech` — Constraints create parallel stacks.

Export controls don't stop capability — they redirect it. Forces innovation along *different* axes. Ask: *catching up on the same stack, or building a different stack?* Catching up means controls fail eventually. Parallel stack means the West loses ability to evaluate Chinese capability through Western metrics. **Both outcomes dangerous in different ways.**

### `energy-systems` — Power is the new bandwidth.

What bandwidth was to the internet (1995–2010), power is to AI (2024–?). When something is the binding constraint, cost rises at the margin, creative substitutions emerge (nuclear restarts, behind-the-meter gas, ammonia-to-power), and winners are determined by who can secure the constraint. **Watch substitutions to gauge how tight the constraint is** — a 20-year nuclear PPA tells you more than any macro statistic.

### `geopolitics-conflicts` — The unipolar moment is ending; we're reverting to historical baseline.

1991–2008 was the anomaly, not the norm. Multipolarity is the historical default — see 1815–1914, 1919–1939, 1947–1989. **The right historical analogies for now are 1900–1914** (rising powers, fragmenting trade, alliance churn, miscalculation risk), not post-2001. Read events through a 1910s lens, not a 1990s lens.

### `industrial-policy-defense` — What was inefficient becomes strategic.

Free-market efficiency optimizes for lowest cost. National security optimizes for capacity to function under stress. These objective functions diverge. **Watch which industries get subsidized**: that's the highest-signal indicator of what's considered strategically vital. The subsidy boundary draws the security boundary.

### `semiconductors` — The bottleneck always moves.

2020: wafer. 2022: HBM. 2024: CoWoS packaging. 2025: power. Whatever the current bottleneck, expect capex to flood in and ease it — and the *next* bottleneck to emerge. **Read TSMC earnings asking: which bottleneck now?** Companies on the new bottleneck see margin expansion; companies on the old one see compression. Missing the chair-change is the most common analyst mistake.

### `tech-investments` — Value migrates up the stack until distribution becomes the moat.

Hardware commoditizes → OS commoditizes → applications commoditize → **distribution endures.** AI is currently in the model commoditization phase. Durable winners own the customer channel — default placements, ecosystem lock-in, workflow integration. **In the AI cycle, ask: who owns the user relationship after the model becomes a commodity?**

## How to use these models

1. **As filters.** When you see a headline, run it through the relevant model first. Does this fit the pattern, or is it breaking it?
2. **As checkpoints.** Once a month, scan the 9 models and ask: *is the framing still right, or has reality moved past it?*
3. **As cross-pillar connectors.** Models share structure — *arms race capex* (ai-compute) and *bottleneck always moves* (semis) are the same physics. Spotting structural overlap is where the latticework comes from.
