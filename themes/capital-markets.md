# Capital Markets Plumbing

## Question
Where is capital actually flowing in the post-ZIRP regime — and what does the rotation between public/private, debt/equity, and asset classes tell us about how risk is being priced and intermediated?

## Why it matters
The plumbing of capital markets is where macro meets reality. Private credit ballooned to ~$2T as banks pulled back. IPOs are scarce, M&A is rate-sensitive, public equity issuance has shifted to buybacks. Understanding *who's lending to whom under what terms* tells you about credit cycle stage, regulatory arbitrage, and where the next blow-up is likely to come from.

## What would change my mind
- **Risk-on / liquidity-easy triggers:** IPO window reopens with size (>$10B in single quarter); credit spreads tighten through fundamentals; M&A volumes surge; private credit funds compete on terms (covenant erosion).
- **Stress triggers:** private credit fund gating; CLO downgrades; meaningful BDC NAV write-downs; a major private credit default event; LBO refi failures.
