# Tech Strategy & Investments

## Question
How are the strategic positions of the dominant tech firms (hyperscalers, foundation model labs, vertical AI applications, semis) shifting as the AI capex cycle plays out — who's building moats, who's being commoditized, and where's value accruing in the stack?

## Why it matters
Strategy in tech right now is more contested than at any time since the cloud transition. The model layer is being commoditized faster than any prior software category. Distribution is increasingly the moat — but distribution costs (default placements, OEM deals) are rising. Hyperscalers are simultaneously customers and competitors of the labs they fund. Vertical AI applications are racing horizontal players. Understanding where value accrues (chips? cloud? models? apps? data?) is the difference between predicting which businesses thrive vs. just observing.

## Mental model

**Value migrates up the stack until distribution becomes the moat.**

In every tech wave: hardware commoditizes → operating systems commoditize → applications commoditize → **distribution endures**. AI is currently in the model commoditization phase. The durable winners will be whoever owns the channel to the customer — default placements, ecosystem lock-in, workflow integration, switching costs in the user relationship. This is why AAPL keeps making 50% margins on a phone (distribution + ecosystem, not chips), why Google search is impossible to displace despite the underlying tech being commoditized, why Salesforce persists despite cheaper open-source alternatives. **In the AI cycle, the question is: who owns the user relationship after the model becomes a commodity?** Foundation labs, hyperscalers, vertical AI apps, OS makers — they're all racing for the same prize: distribution.
