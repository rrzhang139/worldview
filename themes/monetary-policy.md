# Monetary Policy & Rates Regime

## Question
Are we in a structurally higher-rates regime (real r* shifted up due to fiscal dominance, deglobalization, AI capex demand for capital) — or is the post-2008 secular stagnation backdrop still the underlying gravity?

## Why it matters
The level of real rates is the single most important macro variable. It prices every long-duration asset, every leveraged business model, every government's debt sustainability. The post-2008 era was defined by ZIRP; the post-2022 era is testing whether that was a regime or an exception. Getting this right informs how to think about every other theme — equity multiples, real estate, EM debt, credit spreads.

## What would change my mind
- **Higher-for-longer triggers:** core inflation sticks above 2.5% for 4+ quarters; term premium re-emerges visibly (10y-2y spread widening despite Fed cuts); bond auction tails grow; foreign demand for Treasuries weakens.
- **Reversion-to-secular-stagnation triggers:** core inflation back below 2% for 2+ quarters; unemployment rises sharply; productivity growth disappoints; AI capex cycle ends without sustained productivity gain.
