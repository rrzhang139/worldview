# Industrial Policy & Defense

## Question
Is the return of state-led industrial policy (CHIPS Act, IRA, EU Green Deal Industrial Plan, Make in India) producing real reshoring of strategic capacity — or just subsidy capture without structural change?

## Why it matters
For 30 years industrial policy was unfashionable in the West. That ended in 2022. The US, EU, Japan, India are now spending hundreds of billions to relocate semis, batteries, critical minerals, and defense production. Whether this produces real capacity (a TSMC Arizona that actually ships at scale, a US battery supply chain that reduces China dependence) determines how robust the multipolar economy is to conflict — and how much fiscal cost was justified.

## Mental model

**What was inefficient becomes strategic.**

Free-market efficiency optimizes for one variable: lowest cost. National security optimizes for a *different* variable: capacity to function under stress. These objective functions diverge. Industrial policy is the recognition that both matter — that having TSMC concentrated in Taiwan is "efficient" in peacetime and "catastrophic" in conflict. **Watch which industries get subsidized**: that's your highest-signal indicator of what governments consider strategically vital. Currently subsidized at scale: chips, batteries, critical minerals, AI compute, defense industrial base. Notably *not* subsidized: housing, software, retail. **The subsidy boundary draws the security boundary.** When a new industrial policy program drops, ask: *what threat is this protecting against?*
