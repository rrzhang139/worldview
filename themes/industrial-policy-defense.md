# Industrial Policy & Defense

## Question
Is the return of state-led industrial policy (CHIPS Act, IRA, EU Green Deal Industrial Plan, Make in India) producing real reshoring of strategic capacity — or just subsidy capture without structural change?

## Why it matters
For 30 years industrial policy was unfashionable in the West. That ended in 2022. The US, EU, Japan, India are now spending hundreds of billions to relocate semis, batteries, critical minerals, and defense production. Whether this produces real capacity (a TSMC Arizona that actually ships at scale, a US battery supply chain that reduces China dependence) determines how robust the multipolar economy is to conflict — and how much fiscal cost was justified.

## Mental model

**What was inefficient becomes strategic.**

Free-market efficiency optimizes for one variable: lowest cost. National security optimizes for a *different* variable: capacity to function under stress. These objective functions diverge. Industrial policy is the recognition that both matter — that having TSMC concentrated in Taiwan is "efficient" in peacetime and "catastrophic" in conflict. **Watch which industries get subsidized**: that's your highest-signal indicator of what governments consider strategically vital. Currently subsidized at scale: chips, batteries, critical minerals, AI compute, defense industrial base. Notably *not* subsidized: housing, software, retail. **The subsidy boundary draws the security boundary.** When a new industrial policy program drops, ask: *what threat is this protecting against?*

- 2026-07-03/04: **India clears ISM 2.0 (~$13B, up from ~$9B in phase 1)**, explicitly broadening scope from fab subsidies alone to equipment, materials, and indigenous IP; PM Modi inaugurates the CG Semi OSAT facility in Sanand, Gujarat the next day. — First log entry for this theme. A genuine structural signal, not subsidy capture: the scope expansion upstream (equipment/materials/IP) plus an actual *operating* facility — not just an award announcement — is a real capacity data point for "Make in India."
- 2026-06-17: **Coherent breaks ground on a photonics (InP) fab expansion in Sherman, TX**, tied to AI-datacenter demand and backed by a $50M CHIPS Act award (NVIDIA present at the ceremony), aiming to quadruple wafer capacity. — Small-dollar but concrete: actual construction start, directly demand-linked to AI infrastructure rather than speculative subsidy capture — the kind of instance this theme's question is designed to detect.
- 2026-06-03: **EU unveils "Chips Act 2.0"** — proposes letting the European Commission invest directly in cross-border fab projects (beyond its prior state-aid-coordinator role), plus new "Demand Accelerators" linking chipmakers to industrial buyers and a 12-month permitting cap. — A direct policy response to the "subsidy capture without demand" failure mode this theme's question names explicitly — worth tracking whether direct EU equity + demand-side measures actually change fab-siting decisions, or remain aspirational through the legislative process.
