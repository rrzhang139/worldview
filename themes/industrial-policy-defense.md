# Industrial Policy & Defense

## Question
Is the return of state-led industrial policy (CHIPS Act, IRA, EU Green Deal Industrial Plan, Make in India) producing real reshoring of strategic capacity — or just subsidy capture without structural change?

## Why it matters
For 30 years industrial policy was unfashionable in the West. That ended in 2022. The US, EU, Japan, India are now spending hundreds of billions to relocate semis, batteries, critical minerals, and defense production. Whether this produces real capacity (a TSMC Arizona that actually ships at scale, a US battery supply chain that reduces China dependence) determines how robust the multipolar economy is to conflict — and how much fiscal cost was justified.

## What would change my mind
- **Working-as-intended triggers:** TSMC Arizona ships at scale at parity yields; US battery cathode/anode capacity reaches >30% of demand domestically; rare earth processing capacity outside China meaningfully grows; US defense industrial base expands ammo/missile production beyond crisis-level.
- **Subsidy-capture triggers:** plants announced but delayed repeatedly; cost overruns dominate; reliance on Chinese inputs persists; defense primes fail to expand production despite contracts.
