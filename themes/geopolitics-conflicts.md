# Geopolitics & Active Conflicts

## Question
Is the post-1991 unipolar liberal order ending, and what does the multipolar configuration that replaces it actually look like — alliance structures, conflict frequency, trade fragmentation?

## Why it matters
The arc of geopolitics determines whether globalization continues to deepen or fragments into blocs (US/EU/allies vs. China/Russia + non-aligned). That arc shapes supply chains, defense spending, semiconductor policy, sanctions reach, and the dollar's reserve status. Active conflicts (Ukraine, Middle East, Taiwan flashpoints) are the visible tests of the new configuration.

## Mental model

**The unipolar moment is ending; we're reverting to historical baseline.**

1991–2008 was the *anomaly*, not the norm. Multipolarity (multiple competing great powers) is the historical default — see 1815–1914, 1919–1939, 1947–1989. So today's chaos isn't the world breaking; it's the world *reverting* to its mean state. **The right historical analogies for now are 1900–1914** (rising powers, fragmenting trade, alliance churn, arms races, miscalculation risk) **rather than post-2001** (US-led order under stress). Headlines that frame events as "unprecedented" are usually missing this reversion-to-baseline frame. When you read about a new alliance, sanctions package, or arms buildup, ask: *what does this look like through a 1910s lens, not a 1990s lens?*
